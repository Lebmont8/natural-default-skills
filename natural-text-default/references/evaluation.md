# Evaluation and regression checks

Use purpose-built, non-private scenarios. Compare the candidate output with the unedited baseline; do not require a fixed “human” answer.

## Representative scenarios

1. A short message rescheduling a meeting with a familiar colleague.
2. A polite but firm support request about an account verification problem.
3. An Upwork proposal based on three supplied, verifiable project facts.
4. A social post sharing one concrete lesson from a completed task.
5. A service description with a defined audience, deliverable, and limitation.
6. A 30-second spoken script explaining a simple feature.
7. A formal request where legal and factual precision must be preserved.
8. An English client email whose tone is concise and calm.

## Scoring rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2:

- **Factual fidelity:** no invented claims; all required facts preserved.
- **Audience fit:** relationship, channel, and stakes are reflected.
- **Specificity:** concrete details carry the message.
- **Economy:** no removable opening, repetition, or ceremonial conclusion.
- **Natural cadence:** sentence structure follows meaning without forced irregularity.
- **Action clarity:** the reader knows what is asked or what matters.
- **Formatting restraint:** lists, headings, emphasis, and emojis are justified.
- **Voice integrity:** no unauthorized emotion, promise, or persona.

A regression fails if factual fidelity or voice integrity scores below 2, regardless of total score.

## Acceptance criteria

- No scenario loses required facts.
- No scenario gains an unsupported fact, result, feeling, or promise.
- At least six of eight scenarios improve in economy or audience fit without reducing clarity.
- Short artifacts are returned without surrounding explanation unless requested.
- The formal scenario remains precise rather than being casually “humanized.”
- Russian and English soft-signal lists are treated contextually, not as hard bans.

Record disagreements instead of tuning every output toward one universal voice.
